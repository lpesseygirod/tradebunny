How does the council decide?

Your committee's rules, in order. code marks a rule a program enforces; the rest the members are told to follow, and the fact-checker and the CIO hold them to it.

From idea to trade: five stages

Like a real fund: many ideas at the top, few reach the committee, fewer reach your OK. The evening job runs the stages in order; the Pipeline page shows each one.

  1. Scout · every {{ lim.screen_every_days }} days
    Looks beyond the watch list for chokepoints across themes and countries; up to {{ lim.max_new_ideas }} new names. Code turns away any name already held, watched or rejected, or without a real price. code
  2. Research file · up to {{ lim.max_research_files_per_week }} a week
    An analyst writes the homework from annual reports and filings: how it makes money, the five-point test, financials, management, competition, risks, sources. "Send to the committee" shortlists a new idea. Files are rewritten after {{ lim.research_refresh_days }} days.
  3. Committee · {{ lim.max_meetings_per_month }} idea meetings and {{ lim.max_holding_reviews_per_month }} reviews a month
    The full debate on one stock, below. Once a month, a portfolio meeting looks at the whole book and may propose one swap.
  4. Your OK
    Every trade waits for you{{ ' (on the Autopilot book, the council approves itself)' if autopilot }}. Approving re-checks every rule at today's price. code
  5. Watch · every evening
    Results days and one-day falls of {{ (lim.big_drop * 100)|round|int }}% or more on what we own. The sentinel checks them against the thesis breakers: noise, noted, or an urgent committee review.

Where the AI is used, and where it isn't

The AI is used only where reading and judgement are needed. Everything that is arithmetic or a rule is plain code: free, and the same answer every time. A monthly cap of ${{ '%g'|format(lim.monthly_ai_budget_usd) }} per book stops all AI use once it is spent; the rules keep working.

1 · What gets discussed

The council's agenda is picked by rules, never by mood. "Let the council choose" and the daily job use exactly this order.

  1. Urgent reviews the sentinel asked for after results or a big fall. code
  2. Watch-list names whose buy trigger is hit: forward P/E or price at or below the committee's entry level, and holdings at their add level. code
  3. The scout's shortlisted ideas. Names only close to their trigger are listed, not convened. code
  4. Holdings the portfolio meeting sent back for review. code
  5. Holdings not reviewed for {{ lim.review_holdings_every_days }} days: each stock is re-examined about quarterly. code

At most {{ lim.max_meetings_per_month }} idea meetings and {{ lim.max_holding_reviews_per_month }} holding reviews a month, and no name twice within 14 days, so it can't overtrade. code

2 · The five-point business test

"Whoever wins, they must buy from this company." All five must pass for grade A.

  1. Few real suppliers: a monopoly, or an oligopoly of 3–4.
  2. Slow to replace: customers need years to qualify an alternative.
  3. Small cost, critical: a small share of the customer's cost, so price rises stick.
  4. Material to revenue: the theme is a real share of sales (Schaeffler's robots are under 1%: fails).
  5. Self-funded: growth paid from cash flow, not new borrowing.

Then the price test: forward P/E against the company's own 5-year average and its peers, and how it does if rates stay high. A great business at the wrong price goes to the watch list.

3 · One meeting, step by step

  1. Gate code
    Is the name in your mandate, is it not rejected, is a meeting slot free? If not, nobody writes a word and nothing is spent.
  2. Fact sheet code
    Price, forward P/E, 52-week range, what you hold, the caps, the committee's last view. Every number with its source and time; stale prices are refused.
  3. The quant desk code
    Computed from five years of statements and weekly prices: volatility, beta, worst fall, momentum, correlation with the portfolio, revenue growth, margins, debt, return on capital, P/E against its own history and peers, and a reverse DCF (the growth the price assumes). The members argue about these numbers instead of hunting for them.
  4. Round 1 · five blind memos, written in parallel after reading the research file, nobody seeing the others, each researching the web with dated sources:
    Macro strategist: the economy and the AI spending cycle; never names a stock. · Thesis analyst: the five-point test with this round's evidence only. · Valuation analyst: is it priced in, against its own history and peers. · Earnings analyst: the latest results, guidance and call, against the thesis. · Red team: a pre-mortem, "two years on we lost 40%: why?", on a different AI model so its blind spots differ.
  5. Round 2 · cross-examination
    The red team reads the thesis and ranks three objections, each with evidence and a test that would prove it right. Generic objections are not allowed. The thesis analyst gets one rebuttal and must concede where the evidence is against it.
  6. Fact-checker
    Checks the claims the decision rests on against the fact sheet and the sources: confirmed, unsupported or contradicted. Unsupported claims count for nothing.
  7. Risk officer code
    Which holdings are the same bet (correlation of weekly returns, the AI and Taiwan flags), and what each world (markets fall, AI spending cut, rates up, recession, a Taiwan crisis) does to the stock, scaled by its beta, then to the whole portfolio. May only ask for stricter action.
  8. The vote code
    Each member's position after the debate (buy, wait or no), read from what they wrote: their stance, what was conceded, what the price says.
  9. Forecasts
    Each researching member also makes two or three forecasts code can check later (the stock against the index, next quarter's growth and margin). Scored when the answer is known; once a member has five, the CIO sees how calibrated they are.
  10. CIO verdict
    A grade, a decision, a buy trigger and a "sell if" tied to the thesis, with an answer to every objection and to every member who voted otherwise. When the verdict would trade, a second CIO decides independently: if it wouldn't trade, the council doesn't. "No action" is the default: the burden of proof is on buying.
  11. Code checks the verdict code
    Only A, or B with its trigger reached, may be bought. A grade-B name with a price or P/E trigger waits until the trigger is actually reached. A full position needs grade A. A sale needs a named thesis breaker. Then the size: position, Taiwan, AI and stock-count caps, paid from the watch-list reserve first.
  12. Your OK code
    Everything allowed becomes a proposal on the Money page. Nothing trades until you approve. Approving re-checks every rule at today's price; an unanswered proposal expires after {{ lim.proposal_days }} days.
  13. The order code
    Limit orders at the last price, in {{ lim.tranches }} tranches {{ lim.tranche_spacing_days }} days apart: the first now, the rest at the daily review. {{ 'Paper money at real prices.' if mandate.broker.kind == 'paper' else 'Sent to Interactive Brokers.' }}

4 · Grades

  • A
    Buy in stages.
    Passes all five points and is fairly priced. Full position allowed.
  • B
    Buy on a trigger, or hold small.
    A real chokepoint with one open issue: price, debt, missing evidence or a pending event. Bought only once its dated or price trigger is reached, as a starter position.
  • C
    Watch.
    Cheap, but an unresolved business problem. Never bought.
  • D
    Avoid.
    Fails the business test: theme under 1% of sales, negative cash flow, no earnings, a price war, an extreme multiple.

5 · When it sells

  • Only when a thesis breaker fires, named in the verdict. A price drop alone never sells: 20% falls are routine for 5-year positions. code
  • Trim sells half; sell sells all and cancels any tranches still planned. code
  • Every sale is a proposal too, and waits for your OK. code
  • A swap (portfolio meeting) sells a holding only if the committee marked it down (grade C or D, or a breaker fired) and buys only a name a committee already approved. Both halves are re-checked before anything is sold. code
  • If the whole portfolio falls {{ (lim.max_loss * 100)|round|int }}% below its peak, the Rules page flags the committee's rule: move 10 points from stocks to euro bonds.
